- The severe food insecurity prevalence array covers 2018 to 2023 and contains 6 yearly observations.

- Across the severe food insecurity series, the NumPy average is 7.95%, the minimum is 6.9%, the maximum is 8.5%, and the range is 1.6 percentage points.

- Reshaping the six-year prevalence array into two 3-year periods gives a 2018-2020 average of 7.47% and a 2021-2023 average of 8.43%, so the later period is 0.97 percentage points higher.
- In the IPC 2020-10 snapshot, NumPy sums show 59,130,437 people across reported phase rows and 100.0% across reported phase percentages.

- Phase 1 and Phase 2 account for 49,795,278 people, or 84.0%, meaning most classified people were outside crisis-or-worse phases in the IPC snapshot, while 16.0% were still in serious acute food insecurity.

- Phase 5 values are retained as missing values, not treated as zero, because the cleaned source labels them as missing.
